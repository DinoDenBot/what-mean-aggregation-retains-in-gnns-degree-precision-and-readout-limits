# Precision intervention on frozen CSBM classifiers

This is a direct user-authorized local experiment, designed after reviewing the existing transfer results. It is not a Radia-controller run or an independent training replication. The new protocol and source are hashed before generating result-bearing intervention graphs. No external publication or public timestamp is claimed.

Question: does reducing aggregate precision or injecting noise hurt a trained mean classifier, and does a trained mean-plus-degree classifier suffer less additional loss when its degree coordinate remains intact?

Use the existing eight training blocks and their 16 frozen mean/mean-plus-degree checkpoints. Generate eight NEW independent 4,096-node graphs per block at each of lambda=12,24,48, keeping rho=.60 and mu=.35. Graphs and random intervention tensors are paired across architectures. The new graph pool is distinct from the original targets. No target tuning, retraining, checkpoint selection, or hyperparameter search occurs.

The primary intervention acts on the first-layer neighbor mean only, before concatenation with the self state and explicit degree. Its eight coordinates have a common scientifically defined scale across both architectures: the unconditional source message standard deviations, sqrt(1+.35^2) for coordinate one and 1 for the other seven. Gaussian noise standard deviation is c times that vector times 24^(-3/4), with c=.25,.5,1. The primary c=1 standard deviation is about .0922 in the nonsignal coordinates; it is frozen at source degree 24 for all target environments. The source lattice and sampling reference scales are .0417 and .2041. These are only motivating scales: continuous correlated CSBM messages do not satisfy the discrete Poisson theorem, and degrees 12–48 are not an asymptotic validation.

Uniform noise has the same variance as c=1 Gaussian noise (half-width sqrt(3) times its standard deviation). Four independent noise realizations are evaluated per graph and averaged at the graph level. Shared standard-normal draws pair the Gaussian scale sweep and architectures. Uniform draws are also shared across architectures. Empty-neighborhood means remain zero; no noise is applied to them. Explicit degree, self state, weights, nonlinearities, and all subsequent arithmetic remain float32. Float16 and bfloat16 interventions cast completed neighbor means to the named dtype and back to float32. They test output rounding, not low-precision count division or low-precision accumulation. Secondary float16/bfloat16 arms round at both layers. Degree is protected in every arm.

Primary estimands at each of the two degree shifts:

1. Mean harm = loss(mean, Gaussian c=1) - loss(mean, native).
2. Degree protection = mean harm - [loss(mean+degree, Gaussian c=1) - loss(mean+degree, native)].

The protection contrast is also the change in mean-minus-degree advantage caused by the intervention. Positive protection does not alone show that noise harms mean: both quantities are required. The hypothesis is supported only if all four one-sided 98.75% nested-bootstrap lower bounds exceed .001 nats/node. The four tests use a Bonferroni .05/4 allocation; coverage remains approximate with eight training blocks. If support fails and both protection intervals lie wholly within +/-.001, report practically negligible protection in the tested regime. If either protection interval is wholly below -.001, report evidence against protection at that shift. Otherwise report mixed/inconclusive. Execution validity and scientific outcome are separate.

Resample eight training blocks and then eight graphs within each selected block, preserving pairing across architecture, channel, and noise replicates. Use 10,000 replicates. Report ordinary 95% intervals for secondary log-loss, accuracy, Brier, and class-disagreement summaries. Secondary channels and per-seed results are descriptive, with no best-channel selection. Also report a bootstrap over block means as a sensitivity check, because graph and training variability are not independent evidence units.

Validity: verify input and frozen-source hashes; reproduce native predictions from the original implementation, including old-target metric checks on one graph per block and condition; verify that no-op interventions match exactly, rounding occurs on the declared coordinates, empty outputs stay zero, and degree coordinates remain identical. Require finite metrics, all 16 weight hashes unchanged, expected graph/arm cardinalities, and no target-graph seed overlap with the old pools. Store every graph-level measurement, four-noise-replicate measurements, per-layer perturbation RMS, graph seeds, and per-block summaries. A failed validity check invalidates the headline scientific decision.

Interpretation: a positive outcome supports differential precision/noise sensitivity conditional on these frozen models. It does not isolate rational-denominator encoding, prove canonical information loss, or establish that degree is the causal explanation rather than differences in learned representations. A null or negative outcome is informative and is retained without replacing the protocol. Any later experiment needs its own design.

Resources: local CPU, four Torch threads, one process, expected 5–30 minutes and a hard one-hour limit, at most 8 GiB memory and 500 MiB output. Pinned Python 3.12 environment with torch 2.8.0 and numpy 2.1.2; dependencies resolved from the existing offline cache. Inputs are staged synthetic-study code/checkpoints and generated graphs. No execution network or credentials required. Source files, checks, raw results, and a readable analysis are delivered together; the paper is not automatically revised.
